Arbiter: one judge, every kind of evidence

One brain instead of many rules. For each of the ~40 most liquid coins it asks "will this reach +2% before its stop?", long and short. Seven independent kinds of evidence vote; it trades only when the combined odds are clearly above break-even and at least two kinds agree. At most one trade a day; most days it may skip. At +2% half is taken and the rest becomes risk-free; the rest is held while the evidence says the move continues.

Upgrades under test

How to read this page

What the machine does: every hour it invents and tests trading ideas on 6 years of hourly data for about 50 coins. Nearly all of them fail. The few that pass become champions and are traded with paper money (never real money).

The three exams: (1) discovery: the older years, where ideas may be found; (2) validation: the next year, which the idea must also win, and beat random trading; (3) final exam: the newest 4 months, used once. Only then is a rule a champion.

Live vs exam trades: exam trades happened before a rule was promoted (a test). Live trades happened after: the real proof.

"t" numbers: how far a result is from luck. Around 2 is "probably not luck", 3+ is strong. Win rate alone can fool you.

Why trades appear a day late: the data comes from Binance's free archive, published once a day. Each trade still uses the real price of the hour it fired, so the results are exact; they just show up later.

Twins: two rules that trade almost the same moments; only the better one is kept.

Invented building blocks: new measurements and exits the AI designed itself. They're tested like everything else and retired if useless.

When to worry: the status at the top turns red if the machine hasn't run for more than 4 hours.

Paper only. Not financial advice. Most ideas die: that is the machine working honestly.
